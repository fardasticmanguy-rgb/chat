Bio Unit 4 Study Guide: Cells and Energy

Test info: Friday, chapters 8 and 9. Know the equations, where each stage happens, and what goes in and out. Bring a calculator and a pencil.

1. Photosynthesis

  • Happens in the chloroplasts of plant cells (and algae)
  • 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
  • Light reactions: thylakoid membrane, make ATP and NADPH, release O₂ from splitting water
  • Calvin cycle: stroma, uses ATP + NADPH to fix CO₂ into G3P (then glucose)
  • Rate goes up with light, CO₂ and temperature until enzymes denature

2. Cellular respiration

  • C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + ATP
  • Glycolysis (cytoplasm): glucose → 2 pyruvate, net 2 ATP
  • Pyruvate oxidation + Krebs cycle (mitochondrial matrix): makes CO₂, NADH, FADH₂
  • Electron transport chain (inner membrane): about 32 to 34 ATP, O₂ is the final electron acceptor
  • No oxygen → fermentation (lactic acid in muscle, ethanol + CO₂ in yeast), only 2 ATP

4. Enzymes

  • Lower the activation energy, are not used up
  • Active site fits one substrate (induced fit)
  • Temperature and pH change the shape → can denature
  • Competitive inhibitors block the active site

Key terms

ATP
Adenosine triphosphate, the cell's short-term energy currency
Stroma
Fluid inside the chloroplast where the Calvin cycle runs
NADH
Electron carrier that feeds the electron transport chain
Substrate
The molecule an enzyme acts on

Practice questions

  1. Why does the rate of photosynthesis level off at high light intensity?
  2. Where in the cell is most ATP made, and why does that need O₂?
  3. Explain what happens to an enzyme at pH 2 if its optimum is pH 7.
